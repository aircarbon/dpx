# $ACR Token

## Overview

**$ACR** is the native utility token of the DPX platform, providing access to platform services, governance participation, and value alignment. The token is designed to create a utility-driven relationship between platform activity and token demand.

**Contract Address (Avalanche C-Chain):** `0xc0AcD13BD4E568aD96D758DEd3C7150f37e5B016`

**Total Supply:** 1,000,000,000 (1 billion) tokens (fixed maximum)

---

## Token Utility

$ACR serves multiple functions within the DPX ecosystem, with integration deepening as the platform matures.

### Current & Near-Term Utility

**Fee Discounts:**
Users holding or staking $ACR tokens receive reduced SwapBox protocol fees, incentivizing token holding and aligning user interests with platform growth.

**Staking Rewards:**
$ACR stakers receive a share of protocol fees generated by SwapBox trading activity. The staking contract supports multi-token fee distribution (USDT, USDC, DAI), allowing stakers to earn in the stablecoins actually used on the platform.

**Governance Participation:**
$ACR holders can participate in protocol governance through on-chain voting. As the platform transitions to DAO governance, token holders gain increasing influence over protocol decisions.

### Future Utility (Phase 2+)

**Project Proposal Staking:**
Projects seeking to tokenize ERPAs through FctFactory will be required to stake $ACR tokens to submit proposals. This creates quality assurance through economic commitment and generates consistent token demand from project developers.

**Buyback and Burn:**
A portion of SwapBox protocol fees will be allocated to ACR buyback and burn, creating deflationary pressure as platform trading volume increases.

**DAO Voting Rights:**
As governance transitions to the DAO, $ACR holders will vote on:
- Project approvals (which carbon projects can be tokenized)
- Fee parameters (SwapBox fee rates)
- Required ACR stake amounts for project proposals
- Fee allocation between stakers and buyback/burn mechanisms

## Token Architecture

$ACR is implemented as an upgradeable ERC-20 token with governance capabilities, built on OpenZeppelin's battle-tested contract library.

### Core Features

**ERC-20 Standard:**
Full compliance with the ERC-20 standard ensures compatibility with wallets, exchanges, and DeFi protocols across the Avalanche ecosystem.

**ERC20Votes (Governance):**
On-chain voting capabilities with delegation support. Token holders can delegate their voting power to themselves or other addresses, enabling flexible governance participation.

**Permit (EIP-2612):**
Gasless approval functionality via off-chain signatures, improving user experience for staking and trading workflows.

**UUPS Upgradeable:**
The Universal Upgradeable Proxy Standard allows the token contract to evolve—adding features or addressing issues—while preserving all state (balances, approvals, delegation).

### Additional Capabilities

- **Burnable:** Token holders can burn their own tokens; supports buyback-and-burn mechanics
- **Pausable:** Emergency capability to halt transfers if critical issues arise (owner-controlled)
- **Mintable:** Owner can mint tokens (used for initial distribution; total supply is capped)

## Governance Infrastructure

$ACR integrates with established governance tooling for transparent, on-chain decision-making.

### Treasury Management

Platform treasury is managed through **Safe** (formerly Gnosis Safe) multisig wallets, requiring multiple authorized signers for any treasury operation. This ensures no single party can unilaterally access or move treasury funds.

### DAO Integration

The governance system is built on **OpenZeppelin Governor** contracts and integrates with **Tally** for proposal creation, voting, and execution. This provides:

- Transparent proposal lifecycle visible to all token holders
- On-chain vote recording with full audit trail
- Configurable parameters (voting period, quorum, proposal threshold)
- Delegation support for flexible governance participation

### Progressive Decentralization

Governance transitions through stages:

1. **Initial Phase:** Safe multisig controls platform contracts and treasury
2. **Transition Phase:** DAO deployed, governance proposals begin (advisory)
3. **Full DAO:** Contract ownership transferred from multisig to DAO; token holders have binding governance authority

The transition to full DAO governance occurs after sufficient token vesting ensures broad governance participation.

## Staking Mechanics

The ACR staking system is designed to reward long-term holders while generating sustainable yield from actual platform activity.

### Staking Contract Design

The staking contract follows a **Curve-style** design with multi-token fee support:

- Stakers deposit ACR tokens to receive a share of protocol fees
- Fees are distributed in the stablecoins collected (USDC, USDT, DAI)
- No inflationary token emissions—rewards come from real protocol revenue
- Optional fee discount for stakers (e.g., 50% reduction in SwapBox fees)

This design ensures that staking rewards are sustainable and directly tied to platform usage rather than inflationary emissions. In future phases, a portion of collected fees will also participate in token buyback and burn mechanisms, creating additional value accrual for token holders.

## Security Considerations

The $ACR token and associated infrastructure are designed with security best practices to ensure long-term platform success and protect token holder interests:

- **Multisig Controls:** Treasury and critical contract operations require multiple authorized signers, preventing single points of failure
- **Guardian Mechanisms:** Emergency pause capabilities allow rapid response to security incidents if they arise
- **Vesting Enforcement:** Team and investor tokens are locked in on-chain vesting contracts—tokens cannot be accessed before their unlock schedule
- **Controlled Upgradeability:** Token upgrades require governance approval through multisig (initially) and DAO (after transition)
- **External Audits:** Smart contracts undergo security audits by established blockchain security firms before mainnet deployment

These measures reflect the team's commitment to building secure, resilient infrastructure for the long term.
